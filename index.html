Private by design

Your data stays on your side of the glass.

  • No server to send it to. The site is a handful of static files. There is no backend, database or account.
  • Blocked from the network. The page's security policy includes connect-src 'none', so the browser refuses every request it tries to make.
  • Check it yourself. Load this page, switch on Airplane Mode, then open your export. Everything still works.
  • Gone when you say so. Forget my data deletes it from this browser in one tap.

How these numbers are assembled
  • Everything is read straight from your Apple Health export. Nothing is estimated, scored or filled in. Days with no reading stay empty, never zero.
  • Trend line. A weighted average centred on each day: nearby days count most and the weight tapers to zero at the edge of the window, so the line moves smoothly instead of jumping when a single day enters or leaves. The window spans about a week in Month view (±6 days), about a month in 6-month and Year view (±22 days) and about three months in All time (±64 days). Only days with data are averaged; where too few days have data the line breaks rather than guessing. Near the latest date only past days are available, so the last stretch leans on recent data.
  • Faint marks behind the line are the underlying values: each day in Week and Month, weekly averages in 6-month and Year, monthly averages in All time.
  • The strip under each chart is the plain average for each day, week, month or year shown on the axis. Counts show totals.
  • Comparisons are plain averages of a window set against the same-length window just before it. Arrow colour follows what is generally considered better for that measure: green for the better direction, red for the other way, grey where there is no clear better direction or no change. It is a reading aid, not a verdict.
  • Steps, distance, flights, daylight and resting energy are often recorded by several sources at once (Apple Watch, iPhone, other apps). For each hour the single highest source is kept, then hours are added, so nothing is counted twice.
  • Active energy, exercise minutes, stand hours and ring goals are Apple's own daily Activity summaries.
  • Sleep uses Apple Watch sleep stages (iPhone Bedtime records time in bed, not sleep). If an export has no Apple Watch sleep, other apps that record sleep are used instead. A night is dated by the day you woke up. Bedtime and wake time are the first and last minute asleep; the midpoint is halfway between.
  • Sound levels are energy averages (Leq), weighted by how long each sample lasted. Times use the local clock time on each record.
  • Targets and chains. Change the targets in Settings.
  • The one-line explainers summarise widely used guidance (Apple, WHO, AHA). They are general context, not medical advice.
